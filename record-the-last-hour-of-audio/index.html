An app that remembers the last hour — so you don’t have to.

Life Recorder holds the most recent hour of audio in a rolling buffer on your Android phone. Nothing is uploaded and nothing piles up: old audio continuously falls out of the buffer, and a recording exists only when you tap save.

Life Recorder is a free Android app that keeps a rolling one-hour audio buffer on the phone; one tap saves the last minutes after the moment happened. Audio never leaves the device.

How the rolling hour works

While listening, the app compresses audio on the fly and keeps exactly the last 60 minutes on your phone's storage. The save dial shows how much is ready - save the last 5, 10 or any number of minutes back, and the buffer keeps rolling.

Built to run all day

Battery use is engineered down: low-power capture, one write per second, an optional schedule that only listens during chosen hours, and skip-silence so quiet stretches don't consume the buffer. Your battery outlives the buffer.

Private by construction

A permanent hour-by-hour record of your life would be a liability. A self-erasing buffer isn't: what you don't save is gone, and what you do save stays on your device - transcription included.

Questions

How much audio does Life Recorder keep?

A rolling buffer of the last 60 minutes, stored only on the device. Anything older is continuously and permanently discarded. Saved recordings are kept until you delete them.

Does keeping a one-hour buffer drain the battery?

It's built to sip power. You can also set a recording schedule (only listen during chosen hours) and enable skip-silence so quiet stretches aren't stored at all.
